Localized regime shifts in limit order books, detected with space-time Hawkes processes

Student Mamoune El Boukfaoui Supervisor Robert Clements, USF Data Institute Repo Space_time_point_process_research

Summary

Order flow in a limit order book arrives in self-exciting bursts, and its dynamics often change in only one part of the book: liquidity thins on the ask side, or aggressive buying appears a few ticks from the mid. We propose to detect when and where in the book such a change happens, using a neural detector trained on simulated space-time Hawkes processes. Classical likelihood-ratio and scan tests are the baselines. The method is validated on earthquake data with a documented localized change. It is then applied to Binance BTC-USDT order flow, where we measure whether the detector reduces a market maker's adverse selection out of sample.

Research questions

  1. Detection. Does a simulation-trained detector find regime shifts with more power than classical tests at the same false-alarm rate, especially when only part of the book changes?
  2. Localization. Can it estimate the change time and the affected depth range, not only flag that something changed?
  3. Robustness. How does it behave under a misspecified triggering kernel, intraday seasonality and unknown nuisance parameters?
  4. Economic value. Do detected changes line up with known stress events, and does acting on them lower a market maker's adverse-selection losses after fees?

Model: the order book as a space-time process

Each order-book event is a point (time, depth, mark). "Space" is the signed distance from the mid-price in ticks: negative on the bid side, positive on the ask side. The intensity has the same structure as the seismic model, with depth in place of location:

λ(t, d) = μ(d) + Σtj < t K · β e−β(t − tj) · φσ(d − dj) μ(d) baseline order flow at depth d K branching ratio (flow begets flow) β decay rate of excitation σ how far excitation spreads through the book

A localized regime shift is a change in μ, K, β or σ that starts at an unknown time τ and affects only an unknown depth range R. For example, cancellations surge and depth disappears on the ask side between +2 and +6 ticks.

Illustration only. Depth (bars) by distance from the mid in ticks. A localized shift on the ask side, +2 to +6 ticks, thins liquidity there while the rest of the book looks normal. A whole-book volatility measure would dilute it.

Two practical notes on the Binance files

Timestamp units. Binance moved its spot data files from millisecond to microsecond timestamps in 2025. The loader must detect the unit from the date or the magnitude of the values.

Volume. A busy day contains millions of trades. The fitting code already has an O(n) likelihood for the temporal model, and the space-time model will need truncation of distant event pairs.

The motivating finding

On simulated data with local bursts, pooling all events into one time series hides the clustering. A purely temporal test cannot tell the data from Poisson (p = 0.27). A space-time statistic separates them easily: 682 observed close pairs, against 570–949 under the fitted Hawkes model and 169–285 under Poisson. In order-book terms, a whole-book measure would miss a change confined to a few price levels. That is the gap this project targets.

  • scripts/hawkes_basic.py: temporal Hawkes. Thinning simulation, maximum-likelihood fit, standard errors and intervals, likelihood-ratio test against Poisson, residuals.
  • scripts/hawkes_spacetime.py: space-time Hawkes, with spatial and temporal edge corrections, fit checks and the close-pairs bootstrap.
  • scripts/fit_simulated_data.py: parameter recovery across three scenarios.
  • src/stpp/hawkes_temporal.py: O(n) likelihood with an exact gradient, the starting point for real trade data.

Depth as space, and the baselines

Adapt the model to one-dimensional signed depth. Simulate planted localized changes. Implement the baselines: a likelihood-ratio scan over τ, a space-time scan statistic, CUSUM per depth band, and residual tests.

Feb 2027

Neural detector

Train on simulated windows, comparing binned-count and residual-feature inputs. Report power at a 5% false-alarm rate, the error in the estimated change time, and the overlap between the estimated and true depth range. Validate on Oklahoma (D6) and on the dated crypto events.

Mar 2027

Economic value and write-up

Measure fill markouts at 1 s, 10 s and 60 s. Run an Avellaneda–Stoikov-style quoting backtest with and without the detector, out of sample and after fees. Write a 6–8 page paper.
